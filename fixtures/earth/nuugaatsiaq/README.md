# Nuugaatsiaq native ArcticDEM crop

The live Earth example uses a 250 × 250 cell window, 500 × 500 m at the supplier's native 2 m posting, from the downloaded ArcticDEM strip in `demo/research/sources/nuugaatsiaq-20170619/`. `manifest.json` records exact source and output SHA256 checksums, the `[375,375,625,625]` source window, projected grid bounds, URLs, endpoints and worked sample checks. `npm run prepare:editor` uses the bundled GeoTIFF reader to extract this window offline. It never depends on a temporary directory, Python or a new network request. No resampling, smoothing, filling or vertical offset is applied by Hallmark. The upstream strip already includes the supplier's processing, including least-squares filtering/smoothing.

The DEM's native Float32 samples and the UInt8 mask/matchtag bytes are preserved. The three files share EPSG:3413 and the same 2 m affine transform. Heights are WGS84 ellipsoidal surface heights, including buildings. Do not interpret their positive value as proof of land or bare earth. All 62,500 delivered heights are finite. Crop mask counts: 44,088 zero, 17,756 cloud-only (4), 656 water + cloud (6). There are 4,758 interpolated matchtag cells (0) and 57,742 stereo matched cells (1). Mask 1 is an edge bit even though the GeoTIFF also declares it as nodata; matchtag zero is retained as interpolation evidence, not erased as a missing height.

The provider vocabulary retains bits 1=edge, 2=water, 4=cloud and bitwise combinations. The crop contains 0, 4 and 6; the source-wide 2 km crop additionally contains water-only 2. Zero mask is not a guarantee of accuracy. Matchtag remains a separate source-cell record: 1=stereo matched, 0=interpolated. It does not change Hallmark height origins into physical observations, and neither product is a calibrated numeric uncertainty. Planning switches apply only to the supplied bitmask. Selected mask matches form the established lexicographic priority before terrain cost, with no invented quality weight.

The proposed route starts at native row/column (110,100), approximately 71.5352085° N, 53.2140459° W, and ends at (165,80), approximately 71.5341769° N, 53.2147240° W. The initial 76-cell slope route, with either both quality priorities on or both off, lies on the mapped southern settlement peninsula and contains no supplied water-flagged cells. It is an illustrative inspection centreline, not a documented rescue route, road clearance or vehicle capability assessment. Geographic placement was checked against Strzelecki and Jaskólski's [2020 settlement aerial/topographic maps, figures 1 and 3](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/20/2521/2020/), then the [OpenStreetMap coastline snapshot](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map?bbox=-53.225,71.53,-53.198,71.545) downloaded on 5 November 2026. The OSM snapshot and its projected coastline ring are retained as `land-context.osm.xml` and `land-context.json`; these are placement checks, not planner constraints or observations of 2017 road conditions. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

At the goal, the finite height is 28.96875 m, mask 4 (cloud), matchtag 1 (stereo matched). Its Horn slope is approximately 3.77°, with eight cloud-flagged declared neighbours; the centre height is excluded from Horn slope and included by the 25-cell roughness support. **Inspect flagged route inputs** chooses the first affected interior route cell, allowing exclusion and replanning without excluding the endpoint. The exact original source height at (165,78), 28.8984375 m, mask 4 and matchtag 1, is another retained manifest check.

The 17 June 2017 tsunami prompted evacuation and searches under continuing danger, as documented by the [Danish Defence 21 June response report](https://www.forsvaret.dk/da/nyheder/2017/flodbolge-i-uummannaq-fjorden2/). Hallmark's scenario is retrospective: imagery was acquired on 19 June 2017, constituent scenes processed in 2018, the strip created in November 2021, and packaging/publication occurred in 2022. It does not establish this DEM's operational availability in 2017 or documented rover deployment/benefit. The scenario explicitly names knowledge laundering as the danger of losing supplied qualifications through heights → slope / roughness → cost → route, and supports tracing, exclusion and replanning before local approval.
